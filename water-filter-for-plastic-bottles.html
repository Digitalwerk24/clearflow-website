Water Filters That Screw Onto Standard Plastic Bottles

Why one cap filter can fit Dasani, Aquafina, Poland Spring and most US store-brand water.

Published: January 15, 2025 8 min read
Standards PCO 1881 DIN 1881 ISBT

Kurz: Almost every single-use plastic water bottle sold in the US uses the same 28mm PCO 1881 / DIN 1881 neck thread. That means one well-designed cap-style filter can fit Dasani, Aquafina, Poland Spring, Smartwater, and dozens of store brands — no special bottle required.

If you've ever wondered whether you can simply attach a filter to the bottle you already bought at the gas station, the short answer is: yes, if the filter is built for the right thread. This guide explains what that thread is, which brands use it, and what to look for in a water filter for plastic bottles. For the bigger picture on portable options, start with our hub on On-the-Go Water Filtration: Filter Bottles, Caps & Portable Solutions.

Why Bottle Threads Matter More Than You Think

A filter is only useful if it actually screws on. The good news is that the bottled water industry quietly standardized decades ago. In the late 1990s, beverage makers agreed on a common neck design called PCO 1881 (Plastic Closure Only, 1881 series), which mirrors the older European DIN 1881 standard. The thread diameter is roughly 28mm, the height is short, and the finish is designed for tamper-evident caps.

Because PET bottles are blow-molded from preforms, and almost every preform supplier in North America builds to the same neck spec, you can take a cap off an Aquafina and (with the right thread pitch) screw it onto a Dasani. That's the reason a single cap-style filter can be universal — the bottle industry already did the standardization work.

What "28mm PCO 1881" Actually Means

PCO 1881 is the second-generation lightweight standard, replacing the older PCO 1810. The "1881" version is shorter and uses less plastic, which is why almost every brand switched over between 2008 and 2015. Key specs:

  • Outside thread diameter: ~27.4 mm (commonly called "28mm")
  • Thread pitch: 2.7 mm
  • Cap height: ~11.1 mm
  • Tamper band: standardized location for the security ring

A filter built to this spec will thread on smoothly, seal at the top of the neck, and stay leak-tight when you tilt the bottle to drink. If a filter is built for a different thread (like 38mm dairy caps or proprietary sport bottles), it physically will not engage.

How to Check Your Bottle in 10 Seconds

You don't need calipers. Use this quick visual test:

  1. Take off the original cap.
  2. Look at the neck. If you see a short stack of three or four thin threads with a thin tamper ring below, it's almost certainly 28mm PCO 1881.
  3. The neck opening should be roughly the diameter of a US dime (about 17–18 mm internal).
  4. If the bottle has a wide mouth, a sport-cap pull spout, or a square cross-section, it's not a standard thread.

Still not sure? Try the original cap on a different brand's bottle. If it threads on cleanly, both bottles share the standard, and a cap-style filter will fit both.

What a Cap-Style Filter Does (and Doesn't) Do

A water filter for plastic bottles in the cap-style format is small by design. It has to fit in the same space as the original cap. That means it can do one thing well — capture particles down to a specific size — but it isn't a whole-house treatment system. ClearFlow's filter, for example, targets microplastics through a hollow-fiber membrane sized to block particles in the 0.1–0.2 micron range. What a cap filter doesn't do:

  • It doesn't remove chlorine taste — that's a job for activated carbon, which needs more contact time.
  • It doesn't remove dissolved heavy metals like lead or arsenic.
  • It doesn't "purify" tap water to backcountry standards. For that, see Best Water Filter for Hiking and Backpacking 2026.

The honest framing: if you're already buying bottled water and want to reduce the microplastic particles studies have found in those bottles, a cap filter is a precise tool. Why People Search for Brand-Specific Filters

Searches like "filter that fits Dasani" or "filter for Aquafina bottle" usually come from one of three groups:

  • Office and gym buyers who keep cases of bottled water at their desk and want to upgrade without changing routines.
  • Travelers who buy bottled water at airports and hotels and don't want to carry a separate filter bottle. See Filtering Airplane and Airport Water on Long Flights for that use case.

What to Look For When Buying

Not every cap-style product on the market is built to the same standard. Use this checklist before you buy:

  1. Confirmed thread compatibility. The spec sheet should explicitly say 28mm PCO 1881 or DIN 1881.
  2. Pore size disclosed. A reputable maker tells you the membrane micron rating. If they only say "advanced filtration," be skeptical.
  3. Flow rate that doesn't frustrate you. Aim for at least 200 mL per minute under normal squeezing pressure.
  4. BPA-free materials on every plastic component that touches water.
  5. Replaceable or rinseable so the filter doesn't become disposable after a week. See Cap Filter Maintenance: Cleaning, Drying & Lifespan for care.
  6. Clear claims. A filter that promises to remove "99.99% of all contaminants" from a 4-gram cap is overselling.

FAQ

Will a cap filter fit a Dasani bottle?

Yes. Dasani bottles use the industry-standard 28mm PCO 1881 neck thread, which is the same thread used by Aquafina, Poland Spring, Smartwater, and most US store-brand water. Any cap-style filter built to that spec will screw on directly.

What is a 28mm bottle filter?

A 28mm bottle filter is a cap-style water filter built to thread onto bottles with a 28mm PCO 1881 neck — the standard size used across the bottled water industry in the US. It replaces the original cap and filters water as you drink.

Do all plastic water bottles have the same cap size?

Most mainstream still-water brands use the 28mm PCO 1881 standard, so caps are interchangeable. Premium brands like Fiji, VOSS, and some Evian sport bottles use proprietary necks and are not compatible with universal cap accessories.

Does a bottle cap filter remove chlorine or lead?

No. Cap-style filters are designed specifically to capture particulate microplastics through a fine membrane. They do not remove dissolved chlorine, heavy metals like lead, or other dissolved chemicals. For those, you need activated carbon or reverse osmosis systems.

Can I reuse a cap filter on different bottles?

Yes. As long as the bottles share the 28mm PCO 1881 thread, you can unscrew the filter from one bottle and use it on another. Rinse and air-dry the filter between uses to extend its lifespan and prevent biofilm buildup.

Is a cap filter better than a filter pitcher for microplastics?

For microplastics specifically, a fine-membrane cap filter typically blocks smaller particles than a standard pitcher filter, which is built primarily for taste and chlorine reduction. Standard pitcher filters were not designed with sub-micron particles in mind.
